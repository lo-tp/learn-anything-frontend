# Math that a learner reads as text is written as plain text

Two prompts asked for LaTeX — `summarize_step` and `write_questions` were each told "Use LaTeX for math ($ inline, $$ display)" — while no surface that shows their output can typeset it: a Session's step key points, and a question's stem, options, and explanation reach the learner exactly as stored, and nothing in the app renders math for them (#198). A learner was being shown the source of a notation nothing would ever render — measured across the 15 Sessions in a local database, 374 of 553 stored key points and 1,301 of 3,186 question fields contained `$…$` spans — and a lone backslash in that content is consumed as a JSON escape on the way through the structured call, so `\text{…}` arrived as a tab plus "ext" in 8 of 606 stored slide components and `\bar` as a backspace. **Mathematical content destined for a Text surface is written in plain-text math — Unicode operators and letters, and ASCII structures like `x^2`, `v_0`, `a/b`, `sqrt(2)` — and LaTeX is not used for it. Slides keep their own notation, AsciiMath, because the deck is a Rendered surface.** The two notations are separate on purpose: the choice is made per surface, by whether anything typesets the content before a learner reads it, and not per generating stage.

Considered and rejected: keep asking for LaTeX and give those surfaces a renderer — the only option that gets real typesetting into quiz and summary text, but it brings a math renderer and its sanitisation surface into the app for content that is usually a handful of formulas, and it leaves the transport defect live. Encoding backslashes safely end to end (a sentinel codec around the JSON-returning calls, decoded after validation) was rejected for the same reason: it fixes only the class where a backslash is eaten, and the pervasive problem is that the notation being displayed is unreadable, not merely sometimes damaged. Plain text was chosen because it removes both from the source. The cost is real and is deferred rather than dismissed: this product now has no LaTeX to export, and an export or interchange format would have wanted it.

Consequences: content stored before this decision is not rewritten by it, and a Session's inherited concept summaries keep carrying the old notation forward until those steps are generated again; the response-language instruction tells the model to keep mathematical notation exactly as it is, so a Session resumed from older content can still produce LaTeX despite the new rule. Whether the deck's renderer typesets the LaTeX models still write inside slide JSX is unmeasured — 526 of 606 stored components contain a backslash macro despite the rule against it — and that is a question about gating syntax, not about this decision. A record of the notation is kept outside both codebases (this ADR, plus the app's glossary) because the decision spans them: the prompts are the backend's, the surfaces are the app's. Its enforcement is a shared block in the prompt package and a guard test in the backend, so "the prompts disagree with the surfaces" stays a checkable claim rather than folklore. If a review-card export arrives (ADR-0001), the notation question reopens with an interchange cost in it.
